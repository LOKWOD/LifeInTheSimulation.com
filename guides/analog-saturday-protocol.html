FIELD GUIDE 009

The Analog Saturday Protocol

Updated August 17, 2026 · Practical field guide

By Life in the Simulation Editorial Desk

An analog day should not become punishment or performance. It is a weekly experiment in letting time be organized by places, people and physical tasks rather than continuous digital possibility.

Define the boundary Friday night

Choose the start and end time, which devices remain available and what counts as necessary. Navigation, family contact, medical access and emergencies can stay without turning the day into ordinary scrolling.

Tell relevant people how to reach you. Download directions or tickets and write the day's short plan on paper.

Give the morning a physical anchor

Begin with breakfast, a walk, exercise, repair, gardening or reading. Do not start by sitting beside a powered-off phone wondering what is missing.

Keep one notebook for ideas and errands that would normally trigger a search. Most can wait.

Plan connection without platforms

Invite someone, visit a place, call from a basic contact channel or share a meal. The day is not meant to prove independence from people.

Use photographs sparingly and keep them private until later so the experience does not become a content-production challenge.

Prepare for the empty middle

Keep a menu of bounded options: a book, board game, trail, workshop task, cooking project or handwritten letter.

Leave one interval in which boredom is allowed to deliver an idea or reveal fatigue.

Close with review, not bingeing

At the end, note what felt easier, what caused anxiety and which tools were genuinely missed. Check essential messages first.

Avoid compensating with frantic feed consumption. The protocol succeeds when it improves the next week.

Field checklist

  • Boundary written
  • Exceptions defined
  • Contacts informed
  • Directions downloaded
  • Paper plan prepared
  • Morning physical anchor selected
  • Notebook available
  • Social activity arranged
  • Bounded options ready
  • Rebound scrolling avoided

Frequently asked questions

Must the phone be off?

No. It can remain for calls, navigation and emergencies while optional apps stay unused.

Can music be played?

Yes when chosen deliberately.

What if family does not participate?

Set your boundary without policing theirs and choose attractive shared activities.

How often should this happen?

Weekly works for some; monthly may be more realistic. Consistency matters more than purity.

Plan the exceptions

The protocol is stronger when necessary digital use is named in advance. Navigation, care, accessibility, tickets and emergencies can remain available without reopening every optional feed.

  • Write essential plans and contact numbers on paper.
  • Park one charged device in a fixed place.
  • Define the few uses that count as necessary.
  • Prepare physical activities and materials before the day begins.

Review what you missed, what you did not miss and what became easier to notice. Keep the distinction, not the purity test.
