Build an Information Diet

The goal is not less information at any cost. It is a better ratio of signal, context and direct relevance.

45 minutes 3 min read Updated Aug 14, 2026 Ready to run By Life in the Simulation Editorial Desk

PROTOCOL MAP

The goal is not less information at any cost. It is a better ratio of signal, context and direct relevance.

An information diet is not a list of sources you agree with. It is a structure that assigns different jobs to different inputs and prevents breaking news from becoming the default texture of every day.

Setup45 minutes
Maintenance15 minutes weekly
TargetFewer inputs, clearer roles

1. Define four jobs

  • Awareness: what changed that may require attention?
  • Understanding: what provides background, mechanisms and tradeoffs?
  • Practice: what improves a skill or decision you actually make?
  • Discovery: what exposes you to unfamiliar ideas or art?

A single feed rarely performs all four jobs well.

2. Choose one primary source for each job

Select a small number of publications, people, journals or direct data sources. Prefer sources you can visit intentionally rather than only through recommendations.

LIMIT

Start with no more than two sources per job. Scarcity forces clarity.

3. Add a disagreement source

Choose one serious source that interprets an issue differently from you. The goal is not constant exposure to provocation. It is protection against a closed explanatory loop.

4. Set frequency by consequence

Check information as often as its rate of change can affect your action. Weather before travel may deserve immediacy. Most national commentary does not require ten checks a day.

5. Separate news from analysis

Breaking updates tell you that something happened. Analysis attempts to explain why. Consuming analysis before basic facts stabilize often produces elaborate interpretations of details that later change.

6. Create an exit rule

Every session needs a stopping condition: one newsletter, fifteen minutes, the end of an article or a defined question answered. Infinite scroll has no natural ending, so you must provide one.

7. Review weekly

Remove sources that repeatedly create agitation without improving decisions. Keep sources that correct themselves, link evidence and make you more capable after reading.

WEEKLY QUESTION

Which source changed what I understood or did—and which merely changed my mood?

A good information diet leaves room for reality to arrive through people, places and work—not only through media.

END OF FIELD GUIDE 004

Run it once before improving it.

The purpose of a protocol is changed behavior, not a perfect-looking plan. Record what happened, keep the useful part and discard the theater.

Design the route information takes to you

An information diet is not a list of approved opinions. It is a delivery system that separates urgent alerts, deliberate research and background reading so everything does not compete at the same intensity.

  • Keep emergency alerts narrow and tied to real action.
  • Choose a small set of primary or specialist sources for active questions.
  • Move commentary and discovery into scheduled reading windows.
  • Remove one source that repeatedly produces urgency without useful decisions.

Evaluate the system monthly by recall, decisions improved and time spent—not by the volume consumed.
