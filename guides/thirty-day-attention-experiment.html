FIELD GUIDE 007

The 30-Day Attention Experiment

Updated August 17, 2026 · Practical field guide

By Life in the Simulation Editorial Desk

Do not promise permanent digital purity. Run a measured thirty-day experiment: change a few conditions, observe what happens and keep only the practices that produce a more deliberate life.

Days 1–3: establish the baseline

Record screen time, notification count, pickup patterns, sleep timing and the moments when checking occurs automatically. Do not change behavior dramatically yet; learn the shape of the current system.

Name the work, family, training, reading, making or rest that deserves the recovered attention. Attention must be redirected toward something, not merely taken away from a device.

Week 1: remove unearned interruption

Disable promotional, engagement and breaking-news alerts. Keep time-sensitive communication from people and systems that genuinely require action.

Move feeds and shopping off the first screen and establish two or three message windows. The purpose is to make checking a decision rather than a reflex.

Week 2: replace streams with bounded media

Choose one article, episode, playlist or chapter before opening a platform. Stop when the selected object ends instead of accepting an infinite recommendation queue.

Keep a capture list for things to explore later. Curiosity is preserved without allowing every impulse to redirect the current hour.

Week 3: protect one deep block

Schedule one 45- to 90-minute block on most days for the most valuable demanding work. Define the output, remove communication channels and keep a note for distracting thoughts.

End by recording what was completed and the next visible step. A clean restart reduces tomorrow's context-reconstruction cost.

Week 4: reintroduce deliberately

Test one removed app, alert or media habit at a time. Ask what function it serves, what cost returns with it and whether a scheduled version would work better.

At day thirty, compare baseline with actual time, mood, sleep, work and relationships. Keep the smallest durable system rather than declaring a dramatic identity change.

Field checklist

  • Baseline screen data recorded
  • Replacement activities named
  • Nonessential alerts disabled
  • Feeds moved off first screen
  • Message windows chosen
  • Bounded media selected
  • Daily deep block scheduled
  • Distractions captured
  • Weekly review completed
  • Final rules based on evidence

Frequently asked questions

Should social media be deleted?

Only when that serves the experiment. Scheduled or browser-only access may reveal more than total removal.

What if work requires messages?

Separate true emergency channels and test shorter protected blocks.

How is success measured?

Use time data, completed work, sleep, mood and relationship quality.

What happens after day thirty?

Keep what worked and schedule another review in a month.

Pre-register the experiment

Write the rules before the first difficult day. A pre-registered plan prevents you from redefining success whenever the intervention becomes inconvenient or the early results are ambiguous.

  • Choose two outcomes: one behavioral and one experiential.
  • Record a seven-day baseline before changing settings.
  • Change only a few high-leverage defaults at once.
  • Review weekly and preserve exceptions required for work, care or emergencies.

At day thirty, compare the same measures with baseline and decide which changes deserve to become ordinary defaults.
