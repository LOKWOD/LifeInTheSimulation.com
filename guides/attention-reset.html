The Seven-Day Attention Reset

A practical reset that changes cues, creates protected transitions and then reintroduces technology on purpose.

7 days 3 min read Updated Aug 14, 2026 Ready to run

PROTOCOL MAP

A practical reset that changes cues, creates protected transitions and then reintroduces technology on purpose.

This reset is designed for ordinary life. You can keep working, communicating and using technology. The goal is to interrupt automatic loops long enough to notice which tools serve you and which ones merely reopen themselves.

TimeSeven days
DifficultyModerate
RuleChange cues before relying on willpower

Before day one: record a baseline

Without changing anything, write down your total screen time, top three applications and the moments when checking feels most automatic. Estimate rather than obsessing over precision.

Day 1: remove summons

Disable every nonessential notification. Keep calls, direct messages from important people and alerts that protect safety or work. Remove badges from applications that use numbers to create unfinished loops.

Day 2: move the doors

Take high-capture applications off the first screen. Log out of one service you open reflexively. Use the browser version for anything that benefits from extra friction.

NOTICE

Each time your thumb opens the old location, pause. The automatic movement is the habit becoming visible.

Day 3: protect the first and last 20 minutes

Do not begin or end the day inside a feed. Put the device out of reach. Use a physical alarm if needed. The aim is to let intention appear before external priorities arrive.

Day 4: batch shallow input

Choose two or three windows for news, social media and nonurgent email. Outside those windows, close the source completely rather than leaving it available in the background.

Day 5: restore one empty transition

Choose a recurring gap—waiting for coffee, walking to the car, standing in line—and leave it unfilled. Do not optimize the boredom. Let the mind wander.

Day 6: replace, do not merely remove

Put something desirable where the feed used to be: a book, sketchbook, saved long article, small repair, walk or conversation. Empty space is quickly recaptured unless it has an alternative.

Day 7: reintroduce by function

For each application, write the job it performs. Keep it if the function matters and the design is acceptable. Change how you access it if the function matters but the habit does not. Remove it if you cannot name a meaningful job.

DECIDE

I use __________ for __________. I will access it at __________.

Keep three permanent rules

  • No nonessential notification may decide what you do next.
  • At least one daily transition remains screen-free.
  • Every high-capture tool must have a named purpose and a stopping point.

A reset is not a purity test. It is a chance to renegotiate the interface.

END OF FIELD GUIDE 002

Run it once before improving it.

The purpose of a protocol is changed behavior, not a perfect-looking plan. Record what happened, keep the useful part and discard the theater.
