Keep a Decision Journal

A decision journal separates process from luck and turns experience into calibrated judgment.

10 minutes 3 min read Updated Aug 14, 2026 Ready to run By Life in the Simulation Editorial Desk

PROTOCOL MAP

A decision journal separates process from luck and turns experience into calibrated judgment.

After an outcome is known, the path often feels obvious. Memory edits uncertainty out of the story. A decision journal preserves what you believed before luck, hindsight and social pressure changed the narrative.

Time10 minutes per decision
ReviewMonthly or quarterly
Best forMoney, hiring, projects and high-stakes choices

1. State the decision and deadline

Write the choice in one sentence and the last date at which it remains reversible.

2. List the options actually considered

Include “do nothing” when it is available. A decision framed between two active options often hides the status quo.

3. Record your probabilities

Estimate the chance of the most important outcomes. Precision is less important than making uncertainty explicit.

EXAMPLE

60%: project pays back within 12 months. 25%: slow but useful. 15%: abandoned.

4. Name the strongest reason against your choice

This prevents the journal from becoming a sales document written to your future self.

5. Define what would change your mind

Choose a threshold, date or new fact that would trigger review. This protects against endless commitment to a decision whose premises have expired.

6. Record your state

Note sleep, time pressure, mood and who was present. Patterns often appear outside the content of the decision.

7. Review without rewarding luck

A good decision can produce a bad result, and a reckless decision can succeed. Judge whether the process used the best available information, treated uncertainty honestly and matched the stakes.

REVIEW QUESTION

What did I believe correctly, what did I miss, and what should change in the next decision process?

The journal is not a record of being right. It is a tool for becoming less surprised by your own patterns.

END OF FIELD GUIDE 006

Run it once before improving it.

The purpose of a protocol is changed behavior, not a perfect-looking plan. Record what happened, keep the useful part and discard the theater.

Use a record that hindsight cannot rewrite

A useful journal preserves the information available when the decision was made. It should separate process quality from outcome quality, because a good decision can lose and a careless decision can get lucky.

  • State the decision, deadline and options in one sentence.
  • List the three assumptions most likely to change the result.
  • Assign probabilities before the outcome is known.
  • Schedule a review date and score the reasoning before judging the result.

Over time, look for repeated calibration errors: chronic overconfidence, ignored base rates or costs that were consistently left out.
