Friction Is a Feature

Updated August 17, 2026 · Long-form essay

By Life in the Simulation Editorial Desk

Modern design treats friction as an enemy. One click, instant delivery and automatic renewal are presented as pure progress. Yet some forms of difficulty are the guardrails that give a decision time to become deliberate.

Convenience changes more than speed

Removing steps increases completion. That is useful when the action is necessary and understood. It is dangerous when the action is impulsive, expensive or difficult to reverse.

The same design principle that simplifies a prescription refill can also make debt, gambling, outrage and unwanted subscriptions easier to continue.

Effort creates a moment of authorship

Writing a list, walking to a store or waiting overnight gives desire time to be examined. The delay does not guarantee wisdom, but it creates a location where wisdom can enter.

When systems anticipate and execute instantly, the person may experience choices as events that happened to them rather than actions they authored.

Skill depends on productive difficulty

Navigation, memory, craft and judgment weaken when every intermediate step is permanently outsourced. Assistance can expand capability while also removing the practice that made independent capability possible.

The right question is not whether a tool is easier. It is which parts should remain effortful because they contain the learning.

Relationships resist optimization

Friendship and family require scheduling, repetition, misunderstanding and repair. A relationship with no inconvenience is often a service relationship in disguise.

The effort of showing up is not overhead separate from love. It is one of the forms through which love becomes visible.

Design chosen friction

Add a waiting period to nonessential purchases, remove saved payment information, keep distracting apps logged out and require a written reason before abandoning a long project.

Chosen friction should protect values without punishing necessary action. The aim is not to make life harder; it is to make consequential behavior easier to recognize as a choice.

Questions to keep

  • Which convenience repeatedly produces regret?
  • What useful skill am I outsourcing completely?
  • Where would a 24-hour delay improve judgment?
  • Which relationship deserves effort rather than optimization?

Frequently asked questions

Is convenience bad?

No. It saves time and increases access. The issue is whether removing friction also removes reflection, safety or skill.

What is productive difficulty?

Effort that supports learning, attention or commitment rather than arbitrary obstruction.

How can purchase friction be added?

Use a waiting list, remove one-click payment and review purchases at a scheduled time.

Can friction exclude people?

Yes. Accessibility matters. Chosen friction should not be confused with barriers that prevent necessary participation.

Distinguish protective friction from waste

Friction deserves defense only when it protects a real value. A delay that enables reflection differs from a confusing process that merely transfers cost to the least powerful participant.

  • Name the harm the friction is meant to prevent.
  • Check who pays the time, money or cognitive cost.
  • Ask whether a clearer safeguard could protect the same value.
  • Remove the barrier when it survives only because no one owns the inconvenience.

Good friction makes consequential action more deliberate. Bad friction hides responsibility or turns access into endurance.
