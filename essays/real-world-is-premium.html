The Real World Is Becoming a Premium Experience

5 min read

Digital experiences are becoming astonishingly cheap to reproduce. Music, images, games, conversation, education and entertainment can be copied or generated at almost zero marginal cost. Physical reality refuses to cooperate.

A quiet restaurant has limited tables. A trail has weather. A live performance happens once. A handmade object consumes material and time. A conversation with someone who is fully present cannot be scaled.

Scarcity moves

When convincing simulation becomes abundant, unmediated experience may become relatively scarce. We already see hints of this in the premium placed on live events, craftsmanship, travel, private spaces and device-free retreats.

The more perfectly experience can be reproduced, the more valuable unreproducible moments may feel.

This does not mean digital is inferior. A video call can preserve a relationship across an ocean. A simulation can teach a skill safely. An AI tutor can make knowledge available to someone who never had access before.

The point is balance. Cheap abundance is powerful, but humans still inhabit bodies in places. Texture, risk, smell, temperature, silence, eye contact and physical consequence remain difficult to flatten into a screen.

Buy back reality with attention

The most valuable real-world experiences are not necessarily expensive. Walking without headphones. Cooking something slowly. Building an object. Watching a child invent a game. Sitting outside long enough for the light to change.

Direct experience becomes premium the moment your attention stops being automatically available to everything else.
